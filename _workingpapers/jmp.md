Understanding the potential impacts of climate change on agricultural systems requires knowing how quickly producers can reallocate land when adjustments are costly. I address this by developing a dynamic panel framework that accounts for feedback from lagged allocations, leveraging large spatial and temporal variation across the United States over four decades. I use this empirical model to identify the rate of acreage adjustment to persistent changes in climate and to project transition pathways under CMIP6 climate scenarios out to 2050. The framework demonstrates that future crop allocations are determined by the cumulative history of the entire climate pathway. Consequently, traditional static projection methods over-estimate the responsiveness to climate responses in persistent settings.
