We estimate the causal effects of pesticide exposure on prenatal health in California. We assemble an individual-level dataset from 2007 to 2017 linking birth outcomes to fine-scaled daily local pesticide exposure. Using quasi-experimental variation in exposure via pesticide drift determined by location-specific wind direction on the day-of-application, we find downwind exposure leads to adverse impacts on birth weight and gestation duration. The detected effects from pesticide drift are consistent with pesticides that exhibit high volatilization.
